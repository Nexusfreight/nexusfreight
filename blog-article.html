Broker checks

How to check a freight broker before you tender a load

Handing a load to the wrong broker can cost more than the freight: double brokering, a carrier nobody vetted, or a truck that never shows. Five minutes on the FMCSA website tells you most of what you need to know.

Why it matters

A freight broker doesn't haul your load. It arranges it with a motor carrier. That makes two things worth checking: that the broker is licensed and bonded, and that it knows who is actually hauling your freight.

1. Check the broker's authority

Every US property broker needs a USDOT number and broker operating authority (an MC number). Look the company up on the FMCSA SAFER system by name, USDOT or MC number.

  • Entity type should include Broker.
  • Operating status should be authorized, not inactive or revoked.
  • Note how long the authority has been active. A very new authority isn't a crime, but it is a reason to ask more questions.

2. Confirm the $75,000 bond

Brokers must keep a $75,000 surety bond (form BMC-84) or trust fund (BMC-85) on file with FMCSA. It is there so carriers and shippers have recourse if a broker doesn't pay. You can see it in FMCSA's licensing and insurance records for the company.

Quick testIf the broker can't tell you its MC number on the first call, or the bond isn't on file, stop there.

3. Do the details match?

Fraud often uses a real broker's name with someone else's phone and email. Compare what you are given with the FMCSA record:

4. Ask who is hauling it

A good broker names the carrier before pickup: company name, MC number and driver contact. The rate confirmation between broker and carrier should forbid re-brokering, so the truck at your dock is the one you were told about.

If nobody can tell you which carrier is coming, nobody knows.

Red flags

  • A different carrier, truck or driver arrives than the one you were told about.
  • Contact details change in the middle of a load.
  • Pressure to book fast, at a rate well below the market.
  • No written rate confirmation, or one without the carrier's name.

Checklist before you tender

  • Broker authority active on FMCSA
  • BMC-84 bond or BMC-85 trust on file
  • Phone, email and address match the record
  • Carrier named before pickup, with MC number
  • No-re-brokering clause on the rate confirmation
